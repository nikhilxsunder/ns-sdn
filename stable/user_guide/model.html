The model#

NS-SDN represents a scalar series as a trend plus \(K\) sinusoids whose amplitude, instantaneous frequency, phase offset and participation are read off a latent state at every step. It sits between three traditions it borrows from: the state-space model, whose latent state summarizes the past [Durbin and Koopman, 2012]; empirical mode decomposition, whose intrinsic mode functions are amplitude- and frequency-modulated oscillations [Huang et al., 1998]; and implicit neural representations with sinusoidal activations [Sitzmann et al., 2020]. This page states the model as the package implements it, which is the IEEE-ORSS formulation [Sunder and Leon, 2026] of the architecture introduced at FLAIRS [Sunder, 2026].

The state#

A gated recurrent unit [Cho et al., 2014] carries the state,

\[h_t = \mathrm{GRU}_\psi\bigl(h_{t-1},\, x_t\bigr), \qquad x_t = \bigl[\,y_t;\; z_t\,\bigr],\]

where \(y_t\) is the scaled observation, \(z_t\) the optional covariates, and \(h_0\) a learned vector. The state dimension \(d\) is state_dim; the published run uses \(d = 128\). Under transition="timeaware" the cell also receives the step \(\Delta t_t\), so an irregular grid changes the dynamics and not only the phase; under the default "gru" the step enters the phase recursion alone.

Which observation \(x_t\) carries is the alignment. Under "filtered" it is \(y_t\) and the emission at \(t\) describes \(y_t\); under "predicted" it is \(y_{t-1}\) and the emission at \(t\) predicts \(y_t\). The forecasting page has the consequences; the rest of this page holds for both.

The heads#

Five linear maps read the spectral quantities off \(h_t\), each through the link that gives it the right range:

Head

Link

Reading

Trend \(B_t\)

identity

The level the oscillations sit on. Unconstrained.

Amplitude \(A_{t,k}\)

softplus + \(10^{-4}\)

Positive. The amplitude_raw path.

Frequency \(\omega_{t,k}\)

softplus + \(10^{-4}\) (default), or ordered gaps

Radians per unit time. Positive, so the phase always advances.

Offset \(\phi_{t,k}\)

identity

A phase shift the state can apply without touching the frequency.

Gate \(g_{t,k}\)

sigmoid

In \((0, 1)\). Participation of component \(k\) at \(t\).

A learned mixing vector \(w \in \mathbb{R}^K\) completes the emission; it is the implementation note of the IEEE-ORSS paper and the only spectral quantity that does not vary with \(t\). Its sign is what lets a component contribute with either orientation, and its magnitude is folded into the effective_amplitude the spectral state reads power from.

With ordered_frequency=True the frequency head instead emits \(K + 1\) logits, takes a softmax over them, and accumulates the first \(K\) as gaps inside the band \([\omega_{\min}, \omega_{\max}]\):

\[\omega_{t,k} = \omega_{\min} + (\omega_{\max} - \omega_{\min}) \sum_{j \le k} \mathrm{softmax}(r_t)_j,\]

so \(\omega_{t,1} < \cdots < \omega_{t,K}\) at every \(t\) and the components are identified without permutation. The band is \(\omega_{\min} = 10^{-3}\) to the Nyquist frequency \(\pi / \mathrm{median}(\Delta t)\). The option is off by default because the published run did not use it.

The phase#

The phase integrates the instantaneous frequency,

\[\theta_{t,k} = \theta_{t-1,k} + \omega_{t,k}\,\Delta t_t, \qquad \theta_{0,k} \text{ learned},\]

so a component is a chirp: a sinusoid whose frequency the state can move step by step. This is the reason the model can describe non-stationary oscillations with a fixed number of components, and the reason the frequency path is readable as an instantaneous frequency in the sense of the Hilbert spectrum. Under teacher forcing the whole recursion is one cumulative sum, so the forward pass is a single recurrent call rather than a Python loop.

The synthesis#

(1)#\[\hat y_t = B_t + \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k\, g_{t,k}\, A_{t,k}\, \sin\!\bigl(\theta_{t,k} + \phi_{t,k}\bigr).\]

The result exposes each factor: trend is \(B_t\), amplitude is the gated \(g_{t,k} A_{t,k}\) (the paper’s figures), components is the full summand, and emission is \(\hat y_t\). The identity trend + components.sum(axis=1) == emission holds to floating-point precision on every result, and the decomposition page is organised around it.

The objective#

The network is trained on the scaled series by minimizing a prediction loss plus drift penalties,

\[\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_t \ell\bigl(\hat y_t - y_t\bigr) + \lambda_A \,\overline{\bigl(\Delta_t\, g_{t,k} A_{t,k}\bigr)^2} + \lambda_\omega \,\overline{\bigl(\Delta_t\, \omega_{t,k}\bigr)^2} + \cdots,\]

where \(\ell\) is the squared error (objective="mse") or the Huber loss with threshold delta ("huber"), \(\Delta_t\) the first difference along time, and the bar the mean over steps and components. The two penalties shown are the published ones: they are the time-varying-parameter intuition that spectral parameters should move slowly, and they are what keeps the amplitude and frequency paths smooth enough to read. Four further terms are available through Penalties and are off by default:

phase_curvature

Mean squared second difference of \(\theta\), a smoother frequency path by a different route.

trend

Mean squared first difference of \(B_t\), which pushes level movement out of the trend head and into the components.

sparsity

A group penalty \(\sum_k \sqrt{\overline{(g A)^2_{\cdot,k}}}\) that drives whole components to silence, for choosing \(K\).

spectrum

Mean squared difference of the rFFT power spectra of the emission and the target, which rewards the right frequency content independently of its timing.

Every part is reported unweighted in the result’s history so a stage can be read as a trade-off rather than a total.

The schedule#

Training is Adam [Kingma and Ba, 2015] in stages, each a Stage with its own learning rate, step count and, optionally, penalties. The published schedule is

A first stage that fits, a second that smooths. Each step is one gradient through the whole sequence, so the cost of a fit is the sample length times three thousand recurrent passes; the published run on 9,186 daily observations takes well under an hour on a laptop CPU. A fit diverging to a non-finite loss raises NumericalError rather than returning a result.

The published configuration#

The defaults of NSSDN are the IEEE-ORSS DGS10 experiments: \(K = 16\), \(d = 128\), a GRU transition, positive unordered frequencies, gating, the filtered alignment, squared error on a series min-max scaled to \([-1, 1]\), the two-stage schedule above. With no covariates that network has 58,849 trainable parameters, the count of the published run, and the test suite checks it:

import numpy as np
from ns_sdn.spectral_decomposition import NSSDN

model = NSSDN(np.random.default_rng(0).standard_normal(50))
print(model.k, model.state_dim, model.alignment, model.gating, model.ordered_frequency)

16 128 filtered True False

The data of that run are the 10-year Treasury constant-maturity yield (FRED: DGS10) in logs, placed on a daily calendar grid with gaps of up to seven days filled forward, 9,551 observations from February 2000, the last 365 held out. The reproduction example runs exactly that protocol through the package and writes the paper’s metrics table and figures; the DGS10 example runs a shorter version at documentation-build time.
