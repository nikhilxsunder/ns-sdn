Nuances#

Things the model does that are correct, documented, and still surprising on first contact. Each entry says what happens, why, and what to do about it.

The trend head absorbs the series#

A GRU state with a linear trend head can represent the current level of the series almost perfectly, and under the filtered alignment the training target is the current observation. The cheapest way for the network to lower the loss early in training is therefore to route the series through \(B_t\) and leave the sinusoids small, and on short schedules that is what it does: trend tracks the series, the amplitudes are modest, and the frequencies settle wherever the initialization and the penalties leave them rather than at the oscillations in the data. The quickstart shows it on a series with two known sinusoids.

Under the predicted alignment the same mechanism becomes persistence: the trend head reproduces \(y_{t-1}\) and the one-step error is the one-step change. On a synthetic chirp the package’s own tests found essentially none of the oscillatory variance in the components under that alignment, and the trend penalty does not change the outcome on its own.

The published schedule is long (three thousand steps) and penalized precisely so that the amplitude and frequency paths become smooth, readable objects on a series with slow spectral structure; on the DGS10 run the components do carry structure, which is what the IEEE-ORSS figures show. Read any fit’s power_share and oscillation against trend before reading its components, and treat the frequency paths of a short exploratory fit as initialization plus drift, not as a spectrum.

The weekly-fill artifact#

The published DGS10 series is placed on a calendar-day grid and gaps of up to seven days are filled forward, so every weekend is two repeated values. That is a seven-day structure in the input, and \(2\pi / 7 \approx 0.898\) radians per day is close to the lowest frequency the published run reports (0.90 to 0.95). The softplus head initializes near \(\log 2 \approx 0.693\), so a component that drifts up to the weekly frequency is doing cheap work on the fill rather than finding a cycle in yields.

Keep the published grid when reproducing the papers; for new analysis, fit on the business-day series with the integer grid and note that the frequency unit is then radians per trading day. Compare the frequency paths of the two fits before attributing a component to the economy.

Components are identified up to permutation#

Nothing in the default model orders the components, so the index \(k\) is arbitrary: refit with another seed and component 3 may be component 11. The summary’s last note says so. Comparisons across fits must therefore go through permutation-invariant objects, which is what the spectral state is for, or through ordered_frequency=True, which parameterizes the frequencies as increasing and identifies the components by rank. The ordered parameterization is a different model from the published one (it changes the frequency head and the parameter count, and it confines the frequencies to the Nyquist band), so say which you used.

Compression is where disentanglement is weakest#

Two components with the same frequency and offset are one component with a different amplitude; as frequencies converge, the split of power between them is not determined by the data. The spectral-compression reading of 2020 is therefore exactly the regime in which per-component amplitudes and gates are least identified. disentanglement_score and active are the checks, and the measure-level coordinates (centroid, dispersion, effective count) are the quantities that remain meaningful through it.

Two RMSEs, one fit#

Under the filtered alignment the summary reports an Emission RMSE and a One-step RMSE, and they can differ by an order of magnitude. The first is the error of reconstructing \(y_t\) from a state that has seen \(y_t\); the second is the error of carrying that emission forward as the forecast of \(y_{t+1}\). Only the second is a forecast error, only the second enters llf and the criteria, and even it should be read against the random walk. See Forecasting.

Information criteria charge the nominal parameter count#

n_params is the number of trainable weights, which for a weight- decayed, drift-penalized network overstates its effective degrees of freedom, and llf is a Gaussian likelihood concentrated at a penalized optimum rather than a maximum. BIC in particular will prefer the smallest k almost regardless of fit. Use the criteria to rank values of k within one engine configuration and seed, and prefer the holdout one-step error and the spectral-state coordinates for decisions that matter.

Short schedules are exploratory#

Every example in this documentation fits in seconds with a one-stage schedule of tens of steps. That is enough to exercise the API and to see the shapes and identities, and not enough for the components to mean anything. Fits reported in a paper should use the published schedule or a documented alternative, with seed set and a seed sweep for any claim about a particular component.

What reproduces across machines#

The architecture and its parameter count; the validation, the scaling and the alignment logic; the identities between the paths. Trained weights reproduce exactly on one machine with one seed, and approximately across BLAS backends and torch versions. The published 58,849-parameter count is checked by the test suite; the published metrics are reproduced by the reproduction example to the tolerance those differences allow.

Torch threads#

Training is a sequence of small recurrent passes, which gains little from many threads and can lose to contention on shared CI runners. torch.set_num_threads before fitting is the control; two to four threads is usually the fastest setting for the published size.
